Takeaways for VMs

  • Free or nearly free egress is common outside the hyperscalers. OVHcloud Public Cloud and Scaleway include unlimited egress with their instances. UpCloud has none under its fair-use policy. Hetzner EU includes 20 TB and charges ~$1.15/TB after that. Oracle includes 10 TB per month across all OCI services, then charges $8.50/TB.
  • AWS Lightsail is the way to get AWS reliability with included transfer. The $24 plan includes 4 TB, but overage is billed at the full AWS rate of $90/TB.
  • Hetzner is no longer the obvious cheapest option. Two price increases in 2026 raised the 2 vCPU / 4 GB CPX22 from €7.99 to €19.49. The cheaper CX line has shown as not orderable since September. US Hetzner locations include only 1 TB of transfer.
  • GCP e2-medium jumped from $24.46 to about $40/month in recent price trackers. Check it on Google's calculator before you rely on it.

How the numbers were built

  • VMs: the closest on-demand Linux plan in the selected size. For “4 vCPU / 4–8 GB” it's the cheapest plan with 4 vCPU and 4–8 GB RAM, so some are 4 GB (GCP, Oracle, Fly.io) and most are 8 GB; the tooltip names the exact plan. For “2 vCPU / 4 GB” it's the closest 2 vCPU / 4 GB plan. Exceptions: OVH b3-8 has 8 GB because it's the smallest general-purpose Public Cloud flavor I could price, and Contabo's cheapest plan has 4 vCPU / 8 GB. Disk, IPv4 fees and backups are excluded unless noted.
  • Billing filters: “Not billed for compute when stopped” means the VM's CPU/RAM charge stops while it's powered off. Disks and reserved IPs are still billed everywhere. On Azure this needs “Stopped (deallocated)”, not a shutdown from inside the OS. “Hourly, cancel anytime” means billed by the hour or second with no minimum term, so deleting the VM ends the charges. Hetzner, DigitalOcean, Linode, Vultr, Lightsail, OVH and UpCloud's General Purpose plans keep billing while powered off: you have to delete the VM to stop paying.
  • Reliability score (bubble size): my judgment from scale, years in operation, multi-AZ options, SLA and public incident history. 10 = AWS. Around 8 = large, mature platforms (Oracle, Akamai). 6–7.5 = solid developer or EU clouds that have had notable incidents or are single-zone by default. ≤5 = fine for non-critical work, but expect to handle failures yourself. Take it as a rough ordering, not a measurement.
  • Excluded on purpose: single-person and reseller VPS hosts, and anything without an API or firewall or private networking. Contabo is shown as the floor of what qualifies: it has an API, private networks and a firewall, but low reliability.
  • Not verified, check before you rely on it: Exact GCP e2-medium and e2-highcpu-4 prices; OVH d2-8 price (a Sep 2025 listing).
