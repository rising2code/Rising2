S3-compatible storage: storage price vs egress

Public list prices researched in November 2026, in USD. EUR prices were converted at €1 ≈ $1.15. Hover any dot for details. Switch to “My workload” to plot your actual monthly bill instead: the x-axis is what you'd pay for storage (including minimum charges and base fees such as Wasabi's 1 TB minimum or Hetzner's €6.49), and the y-axis is what you'd pay for egress, retrieval and read requests (after free allowances). A dot's x plus y is your total.

Axes

The y-value is what it costs to deliver 1 TB from that tier to the internet: egress fee plus the retrieval fee for that tier. Dashed lines join one provider's tiers. Hover a line to see the cost of reading data back from the colder tier (retrieval fee and minimum storage time). A hollow dot means a free allowance applies first and the dot shows the rate you pay after it. The x-axis is logarithmic, and the y-axis is compressed so the cheap end stays readable.

Takeaways for storage

  • Several providers charge nothing for egress: Cloudflare R2 (since 2022), Tigris, and, since 1 January 2026, OVHcloud Object Storage, which also doesn't charge for API calls. OVH's storage price (~$8/TB) is close to Backblaze's. Wasabi is also free up to 1× your stored data per month.
  • When you store little and serve a lot, Backblaze's 3× allowance stops helping. For example, at 1 TB stored and 10 TB out per month, B2 bills 7 TB of overage. R2, OVH and Tigris charge only for storage. Hetzner (~$1.15/TB) and Akamai ($5/TB) are cheap even though they charge for egress.
  • Tigris is the young one. It was founded in 2021, raised a $25M Series A in October 2025, and runs its own hardware in only a few data centers. R2 (Cloudflare) and OVH have far longer operating records.
  • Hyperscaler archive tiers are cheap to keep and expensive to read. Deep Archive costs ~$1/TB-month, but serving 1 TB from it costs ~$110 (retrieval plus egress).

How the numbers were built

  • Storage x-axis: list $/TB-month for the cheapest common region (us-east-1, us-central1, East US LRS and similar). Tiered hyperscaler storage uses the first 50 TB band.
  • Storage y-axis, list mode: the internet egress rate after any free allowance, plus that tier's per-GB retrieval fee. Request fees are left out in this mode.
  • Read requests (workload mode): number of downloads = egress ÷ average object size. Read prices per million: R2 $0.36 (10M free) / IA $0.90; Tigris $0.50 (100k free); AWS $0.40 / IA $1 / Glacier IR $10; Azure $0.40 / Cool $1 / Cold $10 / Archive $500; Oracle ~$0.34; GCS $0.40 / Nearline $1 / Coldline $10 / Archive $50 (GCS read rates from memory, not re-verified). B2, Wasabi, OVH, Hetzner, DigitalOcean, Akamai, Scaleway, IDrive and Vultr don't charge for reads. Write and list requests are not modeled.
  • “My workload” mode plots monthly dollars: storage bill (including base fees and minimums) on the x-axis, egress + retrieval + read-request bill on the y-axis. Fixed fees that bundle storage and egress (Hetzner, DigitalOcean, Vultr) are counted on the storage side. It applies each provider's real billing rules: tiered egress (AWS, Azure, GCP), free allowances (100 GB on AWS and Azure, 10 TB on Oracle, 3× stored on B2 and IDrive, bundled TBs on DigitalOcean, Akamai, Hetzner and Vultr), minimum charges (Wasabi and IDrive bill at least 1 TB) and base fees. It assumes every egressed byte is read from that tier.
  • Not verified, check before you rely on it: GCS and Azure read-request prices; OCI Archive retrieval fee; Scaleway Glacier minimum duration; OVH Infrequent Access, Active Archive and Cold Archive prices (not plotted).
